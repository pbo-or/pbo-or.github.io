Powell's City of Books

Area A - Downtown Portland

powells.com/locations/powells-city-of-books

A must visit for any first timer to the city. You can't spent just 20 minutes here. You will probably want to return for another visit to see everything you want.

This is the largest independent used and new book store in the country covering a city block. And one of the largest bookstores in the world. This is where I go periodically to escape.

The Pearl Room includes a special access room with antiquarian and rare (high value) books. Interesting to walk thru looking at the selection.

Portland Rose Garden
aka Portland International Rose Test Garden

Area A - Portland West Hills

portland.gov/parks/washington-park-international-rose-test-garden

The Rose Garden has been in existence for over 100 years. The Garden is best viewed when most roses are in bloom early June or so each year (when Rose Festival is happening in downtown Portland).

Located in the hills in West Portland, there are various panorama viewpoints of the city.

The small on-site Rose Garden store is a definite niche store. Everything rose related.

Terrible parking at peak times of day. But worth the walk, if you have to park a ways from the park.

Free admission

Forest Park

Area A - Portland West Hills - 4 miles East of Beaverton

Within the City of Portland, this is one of the largest city parks in the country. About 8 miles long in the west hills above Portland.

Local features include:
  • 70 miles of recreational trails. The Wildwood Trail runs the length of the park with lots of zigzags.
  • Oregon Zoo (largest zoo in the State). This zoo has several unique distinctions making a visit worth consideration.
  • Hoyt Arboretum (smaller park within Forest Park includes 12 miles of trails thru preserved dense forest of Douglas-fir and other trees and wildlife). Map.
  • The Witch's Castle.
  • Various city viewpoints
Access to the above Pittock Mansion, International Rose Test Garden, Japanese Garden.

Mount Angel Abbey

Area A - 33 miles South of Beaverton

During the various times I have visited Mount Angel for the Oktoberfest, I have not visited the nearby Monastery until recently.

Admission is free as is parking.

Google Maps

I found the site impressive:

  • The Abbey site on the highest ground above and south of the town of Mount Angel so there are a few views to take in from various benches.
  • During our visit, we stopped a young man for directions. He was actually a seminary student from Arizona. Found out a few interesting details including: the Abbey is actually a 4 year liberal arts college focused on a Bachelor's in Philosophy (for those heading into the Catholic priesthood). Apparently every 4 years or so, they admit a new cohort of about 90 students.
  • Though a relatively small 'campus', it reminded me of West Point with older brick structures with a solid appearance.
  • After previously seeing some photos, I was intent on visiting the library. Free entry to the public (strict silence enforced). I was very impressed with the modern architecture. Link/photos right.
  • I found it almost surreal to wander the the library and seeing an occasional monk clad in block robe sitting studying materials at a desk. The library content has a unique focus, but I did find a couple of sections of interest to me.
  • The Abbey itself was an impressive church structure (right).
  • In the basement of the Abbey was a very impressive small museum with all sorts of artifacts and stuffed animals. Worth the 20 minutes to wander through.
  • At the north end of the campus is an interesting Abbey store selling various niche items (for a monastery). Akin to the gift store at the Portland Rose Garden (focused on all things roses).
  • In the same building is a nice coffee shop with various tables to sit at. The coffee smelled impressive.
  • Did not see it, but there is a brewery somewhere near the site that sells beer to the public. I understand it is in the town itself (not on Abbey land). Worth another visit in the future to check this out.
A relaxing experience overall. I would describe the refreshing affect of an afternoon wondering about the Monastery as roughly equivalent to visiting the Japanese Garden in the West Hills above downtown Portland (above).

The Gordon House

Area A - 46 miles South of Beaverton

thegordonhouse.org

thecraftsmanbungalow.com/frank-lloyd-wright-gordon-house

The only Frank Lloyd Wright house in Oregon.

It was originally built in Wilsonville overlooking the Willamette River about 21 miles to the north of its current location. When new owners bought the house from the original owners, they wanted to tear it down to build their own house on the parcel. Luckily, a conversancy group got involved to save and move the house in 4 major sections to Silverton in 2001. Since then it has been refurbished and looks impressive.

45 minute tour
$20 per person

Rice Northwest Museum of Rocks and Minerals

Area B - 10 miles NW of Beaverton

ricenorthwestmuseum.org

Worth a visit. Had never previously thought a local rock museum would be that exciting, but I was fairly impressed with the collection.

The museum used to have a neat collection of gold items/rocks. Unfortunately it got stolen twice. Recovered the first time. But not seen since the 2nd theft.

The house it is in is a basic high end craftsman house but with a lot of attention to detail and built-ins. Some years ago, I specifically asked if a knowledgeable staff person could be give me tour of the house main floor (mainly administrative offices). Very interesting.

The museum is now in two separate buildings with a gift shop in the garage.

Admission $18
Seniors $15

Evergreen Aviation and Space Museum

Area B - McMinnville. 28 miles SE of Beaverton

evergreenmuseum.org

Huge selection of aircraft. You will easily spend several hours wandering around.

Recently renamed to "North American Aerospace Museum" (NAAM)

Yes, that is the Howard Hughes Spruce Goose in the photo. Massive.

Visited the Evergreen Air and Space Museum 25 years ago with the boys after the Spuce Goose had arrived. They had a variety of aircraft at the time. Now they have 3 times the number of aircraft in 2 huge buildings.

A retired friend of mine (the former city engineer for Sherwood) is now a volunteer docent giving tours. His speciality area is the Spruce Goose. But, he knows plenty about the rest of the museum. He has offered to provide a guided tour to our guests (max of 4 persons at a time).

Adults $27
Seniors and Veterans $24 (not sure if my friend can provide free entry)

If you want to see everything available, you could probably spend an entire day roaming around this place (including lunch at the cafeteria). During my most recent visit, I found the food selection decent.

Google Maps. About an hour drive from Beaverton.

OREGON COAST - Area C - 50+ miles West of Beaverton (the rough order of the below site boxes is North to South)

North Oregon Coastal Loop Trips


 The yellow North Loop is a car day trip of about 224 miles not including stops at classic sites or choice off-route stops. Often broken down to 2 separate day trips:

North leg (Beaverton to/from Astoria) including Astor Column, Fort Stevens, Fort Clatsop, Seaside and possibly Cannon Beach. Hint: bring binoculars.

South leg (Beaverton to/from Tillamook) including Cannon Beach, coastal viewpoints and Tillamook Creamery.


The yellow South Loop is a car day trip of up to 254 miles. But it is typically taken in shorter destination legs reducing the travel distance for a comfortable day drive.

Example breakdown is direct trip to Lincoln City and Newport and returning the same route.

Astoria

Area C - 69 miles NW of Beaverton

britannica.com/place/Astoria-Oregon

Established in 1812, Astoria is the oldest city in the state and was the first permanent American settlement west of the Rocky Mountains soon after the Lewis and Clark trip (see Fort Clatsop below).

River front trolley, restaurants, a few museums, 20 minute drive to ocean beaches.

This article puts a friendly spin on Astoria:
beachconnection.net

The right photo is taken from the top of the Astor Column (next site box).

Nearby Youngs River Falls is reportedly a very nice site to visit.

I understand that some large cruise ships are starting to dock here for short stops. My guess is that a single large ship disgorging passengers for a day might overwhelm this small city of 10,000 population. Shall see how that works out.

Astoria Column

Area C - In Astoria

astoriacolumn.org

The Astoria Column was built in 1926. 125 feet tall with 164 steps in the circular stairs going to the top. The mural on the outside of the column depicts major history events in Oregon and the region.

Best view of the Astoria area including Columbia River entering the Pacific Ocean (less foggy or rainy days).

$5 parking pass per vehicle

Google Maps

Columbia River Maritime Museum

Area C - In Astoria

crmm.org

Interesting displays of local maritime history and the huge number of ship wrecks in the area. The small bookstore has a respectable collection of local travel books.

In 2026, they will be opening a second museum building (Mariners Hall). Looks mostly related to Native American history.

Adults (18+) $16
Seniors (65+) $13

Includes admission to the Columbia Lightship docked right outside on the Columbia River. Self-guided tour.

The active duty US Coast Guard cutter serving the Northern Oregon coast area is docked at the same pier as the Lightship.

Article about the museum.

Fort Stevens State Park

Area C - 6 miles West of Astoria

stateparks.oregon.gov

State Park including campground, beach access, coastal and Columbia River viewpoints (below), museum, concrete bunkers and gun emplacements (right). Fort Stevens was a former fort defending the end of the Columbia River at the Pacific from Japanese submarines. In 1942 an enemy sub actually fired upon the fort. They missed their target a long ways off. They were aiming at a supposed submarine base on the other side of Astoria (that was never built) and hit ground 10 miles away. Good intelligence from High Command!

$5 day-use pass per car



The old military post housing is now a public residential area, but in a drive-thru you can see the outlines of the former officer housing and parade ground.

In 1906 the Peter Iredale ran aground at the beach. No loss of life, but the ship was abandoned as a loss. For over a century sands have shifted with the tides and storms. Recently in October 2023, much of the ship's steel hull was exposed. In looking at this photo, consider that in the 1970's I remember visiting the site to only see only a foot of the top of the bow of the ship exposed in the sand. Gives a flavor for how much the beach shifts around over time.

Tillamook Air Museum
Former Tillamook Naval Air Station (aka Blimp Base)

Area C - 50 miles West of Beaverton (about 5 minutes south of Tillamook)

nps.gov/articles/us-naval-air-station-dirigible-hangar-b

During WWII, Tillamook Naval Air Station headquartered various blimps to conduct anti-submarine patrols along the Oregon Coast. There used to be two big blimp hangers here. They were acknowledged to be among the largest wooden structures in the world. After the war, the Air Station closed.

Fall of 1992, Blimp Hanger A burned down. At the time we were renting a house about 3 miles away. Mary had just got off her late hospital shift at 11pm and arrived home to tell me about it as she could see the fire directly down the highway from the house. I immediately drove down Hwy 101 and parked to observe the raging fire. It was so hot that sitting in my pickup about a mile away, I could feel the intense heat thru the closed window.

Soon afterwards, the Blimp Hanger B opened the Tillamook Air Museum. An interesting visit to learn some local history. The hanger is huge and always a very cool temperature inside. Various aircraft on display.

Along with the museum, various local industrial businesses operate in and around the hanger on the former base.

Right is a photo from the war.

SOUTHERN OREGON/NORTHERN CALIFORNIA - Area F - 250+ miles S of Beaverton

Crater Lake National Park

Area F - 180 miles South of Beaverton

nps.gov/crla

Mount Mazama was typical of mountains in the Cascades. Then during a tremendous eruption about 8,000 years ago, Mazama collapsed into itself. The eruption formed a large caldera that eventually formed Crater Lake.

No inlets or outlets; only rain and snowmelt keep it full. The National Park Service does not allow power boats in the lake. Probably one of the clearest, blue lakes you will find. America’s deepest lake at 1,943 feet

Rim Drive circles the caldera for 33 miles, passing 30 named overlooks.

The only National Park in Oregon.

The site is inaccessible and closed during the Winter due to accumulated snow. It reopens late Spring when the roads can finally be cleared for the public.

Above photo was taken during our June 2024 road trip to Eastern OR. They had just opened the park after clearing snow from the entry roads.

A 1998 photo of Mary and the boys at Crater Lake

The ferries are used daily for commuters who live on the islands and Port Townsend to commute to/from Seattle for their jobs. Kind of like commuting on the bus or subway in a big city. A pricey but, different kind of life style.

Tickets (cost varies). ~$13/car+driver plus ~$5 per passenger (unfortunately this is per trip... not a daily pass).

Probably expensive, but an experience you will not forget.

Be prepared to dress warmly.

I have read about a few tour boat businesses that will refund the day trip cost if you don't see any orcas that day.

There are some whale watching partial day trips available on the Oregon Coast. But that is mostly seasonal during the grey whale migrations.

More details about Fern Canyon

7/22/12 Spent some time trying to describe to a friend at work Fern Canyon just outside of the Redwood National Park in CA and could not do it justice. Decided to post some photos from our 2002 & 2009 trips through the area:

Mary
Max
Me & Mary
The family

This state park is a hidden jewel at the perimeter of the Redwood National Park. It is located right next to the beach. A couple mile drive West from Hwy 101 on a gravel road under heavy cover canopy. Have to ford at least one small creek with your car. The 1+ mile hiking trail up Fern Canyon starts at the beach.

Watch out for the wildlife walking to the trailhead from the parking lot. Incredible. Well worth the 2-3 hour stopover visit while heading North from CA to OR.

From the Hwy 101 turnoff to the last parking lot is about 7 miles. This access road has some very thick tree cover so relying on a Smartphone GPS for most of the road trip might not be reliable. The drive itself is worth a leisurely pace. Count on need for a car wash after the visit (dust).

Admission to the park is now something like $8 (worth the admission). The canyon entrance is about 1/4 mile north of the last north beach parking lot.

Make sure to go on a mid-week day to avoid any crowds.

A late note: if you think I cherry picked my best pics of Fern Canyon for this web page, look over a Google pic search of the location. Every pic is breathtaking.
